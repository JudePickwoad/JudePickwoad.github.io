Face ID Smart Lock - Electrical Engineering Capstone Project

For my capstone project, I worked with a team of five students: two electrical and three computer engineering students to design and develop a contactless smart lock with facial recognition as its primary security feature. Over two semesters, we researched and developed a solution tailored for homeowners with mobility and dexterity challenges. The lock ensures ease of access without physical keys or PIN codes.

As the CAD designer, I was responsible for designing the lock’s outer shell and optimizing it for functionality and aesthetics. I created two alternate designs, and the final prototype was 3D-printed using plastic to test its feasibility. The smart lock used a Raspberry Pi and Raspberry Pi Camera, that provided real-time facial recognition processing. It was designed to be battery-operated, making it wireless and easy to install. Additionally, we developed a web-based companion app that allowed users to manage access remotely, adding an extra layer of convenience. For added security and emergency access, the system also featured a deadbolt lock that could be opened with a physical key.

This project was a defining experience in my engineering education, strengthening my skills in hardware integration, CAD modelling, and collaborative problem-solving while applying cutting-edge technology to real-world accessibility challenges.

Pet Weather Monitoring System

Newfoundland’s harsh winters and unpredictable weather challenge pet owners, often limiting outdoor activities and posing safety risks. Conventional weather apps provide generalized forecasts that may not account for localized microclimates. To address this, my partner and I developed a personalized weather data logger providing real-time, location-specific weather insights tailored to pet owners' needs.

The system integrates sensors to measure temperature, humidity, and rainfall. Signal processing ensures accuracy, and data is transmitted via Bluetooth to a mobile app that allows monitoring and gives recommendations. This improves pet safety and helps owners make better decisions based on microclimate data.

Metocean Study

During my first work term at C-CORE, I contributed to updating the Metocean study using oceanographic and environmental data from 2017–2021. Our analysis spanned maritime regions from 39.5°N to 63°N and 42°W to 65°W. We used ArcGIS Pro and Sentinel-1 satellite imagery to track iceberg distribution, sea ice coverage, and ship density. This project enhanced my geospatial data processing and remote sensing skills.
